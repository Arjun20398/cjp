# Cheap Justice of India — Site Plan

> A satirical website exposing the rot in India's judiciary through humor, facts, and fearless commentary.

---

## Tagline Ideas

- *"Because justice shouldn't come with a price tag"*
- *"Holding the bench accountable, one verdict at a time"*
- *"They judge you. We judge them."*

---

## Core Content Pillars

### 1. The Corruption Files

Exposing financial and ethical corruption within the judiciary.

- Judges with disproportionate assets
- Land allotment scams involving judges and their families
- Benami properties and shell companies linked to judicial officers
- Quid pro quo — favorable verdicts in exchange for post-retirement benefits
- Nepotism in judicial appointments (judges appointing judges' children as lawyers, clerks, etc.)

### 2. Loophole Engineers

How judges exploit and create legal loopholes.

- Vague or deliberately ambiguous judgments that leave room for re-litigation
- Frequent adjournments that benefit the wealthy party
- Sealed cover jurisprudence — hiding evidence from the public in the name of "national interest"
- Suo motu cases selectively picked to serve political interests
- Selective application of contempt of court to silence critics

### 3. Flawed Judgments Hall of Fame

A satirical archive of the most absurd, contradictory, or harmful judgments.

- Judgments that contradict each other from the same bench
- Moral policing disguised as legal reasoning
- Verdicts where the reasoning defies basic logic or constitutional principles
- Cases where the powerful got away and the powerless got crushed
- Delayed justice that amounted to no justice (decades-long pending cases)

### 4. The Revolving Door — Judges in Politics

Post-retirement careers that expose pre-retirement bias.

- Judges who joined political parties immediately after retirement
- Governors, Rajya Sabha MPs, and tribunal heads — the retirement reward system
- Timeline comparisons: controversial pro-government verdicts vs. cushy post-retirement postings
- The conflict of interest nobody talks about
- How the "cooling off period" is a joke

### 5. The Collegium Circus

India's opaque system of judicial appointments.

- How the collegium picks judges behind closed doors
- Deserving candidates rejected, connected candidates elevated
- Transfer racket — punishing independent judges by transferring them
- The government-judiciary tug of war over appointments (and how both sides lose the public's trust)

### 6. Contempt of Common Sense

Satirical opinion pieces and commentary.

- Open letters to the judiciary (written in a satirical tone)
- "What if judges were judged?" — hypothetical accountability scenarios
- Parody of judicial language and legalese
- Satirical awards: "Loophole of the Month", "Verdict That Made No Sense Award"
- Comparing Indian judiciary with judiciary systems in other democracies

## Tone & Voice

- **Satirical but fact-backed** — every claim supported by public records, RTI data, or credible news sources
- **Irreverent but not defamatory** — mock the system, not individuals without evidence
- **Accessible** — no unnecessary legalese; written for the common citizen, not lawyers
- **Fearless** — if it's public record, it's fair game

---

## Legal Safeguards

- All content clearly labeled as **satire / opinion** where applicable
- Factual claims backed by **cited sources** (court records, RTI responses, news reports)
- Prominent **disclaimer** on the site: *"This is a satirical publication. All factual claims are sourced from publicly available records."*
- No content targeting judges' personal lives — only their **professional conduct and public actions**
- Consult a media lawyer before publishing sensitive pieces

---

## Key Data Points to Track & Publish

- Number of cases pending in each court
- Average time to judgment across courts
- Post-retirement appointments of Supreme Court and High Court judges
- Exposed assets of judges (from public declarations)
- Collegium recommendations vs. government approvals (acceptance/rejection rates)
- Contempt of court cases — who was targeted and why
